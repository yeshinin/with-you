For patients & caregivers

Most care plans fail at home. With You is there when it’s time to act.

A voice-first guide for patients and caregivers after a hospital stay. It calls when each medicine or task is due, walks you through it, spots problems like a missing ride or refill before care is missed, and makes sure every question gets answered.

Technology designed to reduce disparity in support: across language, health literacy, everyday circumstances and comfort with technology.

Care continues at home

Behind the care plan is someone learning how to make it work on their own.

  1. 1 in 4 U.S. adults, about 63 million people, are now family caregivers. That’s up 45% in a decade.1
  2. 55% of them perform medical or nursing tasks: medicines, wound care, monitoring, medical equipment.1
  3. Only 22% of those performing these tasks report receiving training.1
  4. 1 in 5 patients experience an adverse event after leaving the hospital.2

When the care plan meets real life

Going home is a milestone. Figuring out life at home is a journey.

Medicines, things to watch out for, learning unfamiliar tasks, and questions that surface along the way. Here’s how support can change the experience.

Without support

  1. Discharge dayHome with more questions than answers.A folder of instructions, a hurried explanation, and unfamiliar medical terms in a language they’re less comfortable with.
  2. Day 2Five new medicines.Which need food? New things to log and monitor. The caregiver searches the paperwork again.
  3. Day 4A new worry, late at night.The wound looks different during a dressing change, and they’re unsure what it means. The drain looks clogged, and they’re not sure how to fix it.
  4. Day 6No ride to the follow-up appointment.It quietly gets missed.
  5. Day 12So much to say, so little time.During a brief visit, they struggle to recall the past week. The concern they most wanted to discuss comes back to them on the way home.

With You

  1. Before leavingThe plan is explained in their language. The caregiver practises the dressing change before going home, and visual guides that go home with them help the caregiver feel ready.
  2. Day 2A gentle voice walks through the morning medicines, one at a time, waits for confirmation, and asks what feels difficult. Fever and drain output are logged by voice.
  3. Day 4A tailored, clinician-reviewed timeline explains what to expect, from wound care to bathing and sleep, with clear instructions on when and whom to call. Verified guides are there to remind them of what they learned.
  4. Day 5Transport needs are checked early, with help arranging a ride. Practical barriers surface while there’s still time to act.
  5. Day 12Their spoken notes become a clear summary and priority questions, ready to make the most of a short visit.
  6. As recovery continuesPeer connections and stories offer support at each milestone, as they return to everyday life and find their own new normal.

How it works

Six ways With You stays with you.

Read itSee itHear itIn your language
01 · Understand

Your care, in words you understand.

Plain language, at your pace, with the warning signs that matter for you.

02 · Care at home

Talked through every task.

Voice prompts and visual aids, day by day. When a problem needs a person, a trained community health worker steps in.

03 · Prepare

Know what’s ahead.

What to expect week by week, from everyday routines to bigger milestones, reviewed by clinicians.

04 · Barriers

Obstacles caught early.

No ride? No refill? Help is lined up before it becomes a problem.

05 · Visits

Ready for every visit.

Your voice notes become a clear summary and the questions to ask.

06 · Connection

Never on your own.

Peers who’ve been through it, their journey at each milestone, for as long as you need.

Clinical guidance comes from your care team and is reviewed by qualified people. Peer stories are always labelled as experience, not medical advice.
